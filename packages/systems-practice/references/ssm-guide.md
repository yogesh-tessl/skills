## Core Idea

Soft Systems Methodology, developed by Peter Checkland, starts from the premise that in human activity systems, there is no single "correct" problem definition. Different stakeholders experience the situation differently because they hold different worldviews (Weltanschauungen).

SSM does not try to resolve these differences by declaring one worldview "right." Instead, it makes the differences explicit, then uses structured debate to find accommodations — actions that different worldviews can live with, even if they don't fully agree.

The key insight: **the problem is not "out there" waiting to be discovered — it is constructed by whoever is framing it.** Different framings lead to different interventions, and choosing a framing is itself a political act.

## Rich Pictures

Before building CATWOE tables, it helps to draw (or describe) a Rich Picture — an informal sketch of the situation that captures:

- **Actors** and their relationships
- **Processes** and flows
- **Conflicts** and tensions (crossed swords, question marks)
- **Concerns** and worries (thought bubbles)
- **Power dynamics** (who is big/central vs. small/peripheral)

Rich Pictures are deliberately informal. They are not diagrams with precise notation — they are thinking tools that help surface what a formal model might miss. When working in text, describe the key elements that would appear in a Rich Picture.

## Root Definitions & CATWOE

A Root Definition is a concise statement of a purposeful human activity system, written from a specific worldview. The formula:

> "A system owned by **O** in which **A** perform **T** for **C**, based on the belief that **W**, subject to **E**."

### Building a Good CATWOE

| Element | Key Question | Common Mistake |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **C** — Customers | Who would be the first to notice if this system ceased to exist? | Confusing customers with actors. Customers receive the output; actors produce it. |
| **A** — Actors | Who does the work? | Listing organizations instead of roles. Be specific about who actually performs activities. |
| **T** — Transformation | What is the input state → output state? | Writing an activity instead of a transformation. "Train staff" is an activity; "Untrained staff → Competent staff" is a transformation. |
| **W** — Worldview | What belief makes this transformation worth doing? | Being too vague. "It's important to help people" is not a worldview. "Early intervention reduces long-term costs by 60% and improves outcomes" is. |
| **O** — Owner | Who could abolish this system? | Listing too many owners. There is usually one person or body with ultimate authority. |
| **E** — Environment | What constraints are taken as given? | Listing things that could actually be changed. Environment means genuine constraints: legal requirements, laws of physics, budget ceilings set externally. |

### The Transformation Test

The most important element is **T**. Test your transformation by checking:

1. Is it expressed as an input → output change of state? (not an activity)
2. Is the input logically connected to the output? (you can trace the change)
3. Could someone disagree that this transformation is desirable? (if no one could disagree, you may be stating something trivially true)

### Multiple Root Definitions

For a messy situation, build 2-3 Root Definitions from different worldviews. The power of SSM comes from comparing them:

- Where do the Transformations differ? (different groups want different changes)
- Where do the Worldviews conflict? (different beliefs about what matters)
- Where do the Environments differ? (different groups face different constraints)

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Treating CATWOE as a form to fill in** rather than a thinking tool. The value is in the discussion each element provokes, not in completing the table.

2. **Writing only one Root Definition.** One RD captures one worldview. If you have only one, you're not doing systems practice — you're doing conventional analysis with extra steps.

3. **Confusing T with a solution.** "Implement a new CRM system" is a solution. "Customer needs poorly understood → Customer needs systematically captured and acted on" is a transformation. The transformation is what you want to achieve; the solution is how.

4. **Skipping W.** Worldview is what makes different RDs genuinely different. Two RDs with the same W are probably the same RD dressed differently.

5. **Making E too broad.** "The current economic climate" is not useful. "Annual budget capped at $2M by board resolution" is.

## Worked Example

**Situation**: A hospital wants to reduce patient waiting times in the emergency department.

### Root Definition 1 (Management perspective)

> "A system owned by the Hospital CEO in which ED managers and triage nurses transform long, unpredictable waiting times into standardized, target-compliant wait times for patients, based on the belief that operational efficiency and measurable targets drive quality improvement, subject to government-mandated staffing ratios and fixed annual budgets."

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| C | Patients waiting in ED |
| A | ED managers, triage nurses |
| T | Long, unpredictable waits → Standardized, target-compliant waits |
| W | Operational efficiency and measurable targets drive quality |
| O | Hospital CEO |
| E | Government staffing ratios, fixed budgets |

### Root Definition 2 (Clinical staff perspective)

> "A system owned by the Chief Medical Officer in which emergency physicians and nurses transform patients presenting with undifferentiated symptoms into patients who have received timely, clinically appropriate assessment, based on the belief that clinical judgment and adequate resources — not throughput targets — determine patient safety, subject to unpredictable patient volumes and acuity."

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| C | Patients presenting with undifferentiated symptoms |
| A | Emergency physicians, nurses |
| T | Undifferentiated presentation → Timely, clinically appropriate assessment |
| W | Clinical judgment and adequate resources determine safety, not throughput targets |
| O | Chief Medical Officer |
| E | Unpredictable patient volume and acuity |

### Conflict Summary

| Management framing | Clinical framing | Tension |
|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| Problem is inefficiency | Problem is under-resourcing | Different root causes imply different solutions |
| Success = hitting wait-time targets | Success = safe clinical outcomes | Targets may incentivize rushing assessments |
| Constraint = fixed budget | Constraint = unpredictable demand | One sees budget as immovable; the other sees it as the problem |

This conflict cannot be "resolved" by analysis — it requires negotiation and accommodation. The value of making it explicit is that both sides can see the other's logic, rather than dismissing it as ignorance or stubbornness.
